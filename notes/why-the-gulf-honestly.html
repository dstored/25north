Why the Gulf, honestly

The case for moving your career to the UAE, including the parts the brochures leave out, because you’re a senior operator and you can smell a brochure.

November 2026 · Opinion, clearly labelled as such

You’ve seen the LinkedIn posts. Skyline, sunrise, “best decision I ever made,” a rocket emoji. You’ve also seen the replies underneath from people who think the whole place is a mirage with a conference centre attached. Both camps are selling you a simple story, and at your level simple stories should make you suspicious. Here is the complicated one.

What’s actually true

The capital is real. This isn’t 2015’s oil money looking for a hobby. Venture funds headquartered in Dubai and Abu Dhabi are raising nine-figure funds and deploying them locally; family offices are building companies, not just buying buildings; and the regulators at DIFC and ADGM have built genuinely credible licensing regimes that UK financial firms now enter on purpose. Companies you haven’t heard of yet are raising $30–70m rounds in Dubai, and their biggest constraint, openly stated in their own funding announcements, is senior people.

The seniority arrives faster. This is the under-priced part. In London you are one of forty credible candidates for every big job, and the big job is a narrow slice of a mature org. In the Gulf, a proven UK operator walks into regional remit: the P&L, the regulator relationship, the build-it-from-scratch mandate that London would make you wait a decade for. People don’t stay for the tax. They stay because they got five years of career in two.

The logistics are easier than you fear. Golden visas for senior professionals, employer-sponsored everything, English as the working language, direct flights to every UK airport that matters. The hard part of the move is the decision, not the paperwork. The paperwork is what employers (and we) handle.

What’s also true

It is not a liberal democracy, and you shouldn’t pretend otherwise. There is no serious political speech, the press is managed, and public criticism of the state is off the table. Daily professional life, for almost everyone, is unremarkable and safe, noticeably safer than London in the ways you feel day to day. But if public political expression is a load-bearing part of your life, that is a real cost, and you should weigh it as one. Some people should not make this move, and it’s better to know that in London than discover it in August in Dubai.

August is a furnace. Four months of the year, life happens indoors. Families structure summers around being elsewhere; employers know this and senior packages assume it. It sounds trivial until you live it, so don’t let anyone tell you it’s nothing.

The noise is real too. For every funded, licensed, properly governed company there is a glossy one running on a press release and a prayer. The ecosystem has hype because hype is cheap and capital is loud. Telling the two apart before you sign, not after, is a skill. Frankly, it is part of what the network is for. Ask who the investors are, where the licence is, and who the last senior hire was and whether they stayed.

Distance costs something. Ageing parents, old friends, the pub on a Thursday. Direct flights shrink the distance; they don’t delete it. The people who do this well go in with their eyes open about what a seven-hour flight means when something happens at home.

Who thrives, who shouldn’t come

The pattern across the people who make this work: they came for a specific role at a specific company they’d diligenced hard, they moved with their family’s full buy-in rather than over it, they treated the tax as compounding rather than the point, and they joined a peer network fast. The pattern across the people who left early: they came for money alone, they took the first offer from the glossiest logo, or they never got the family settled.

If you need political life, hate heat, or are running from something rather than to something: stay. Genuinely. A placement that fails costs us the fee and you two years; we have no interest in talking you into anything.
