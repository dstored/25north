The maths of leaving

What a UK salary actually becomes in Dubai: tax, rent, schools and pensions included. If the move doesn’t work on paper, it won’t work in person.

November 2026 · UK figures use 2025/26 tax bands · AED figures at £1 ≈ AED 4.6, marked approximate where they are

Start with the part everyone quotes at dinner parties, because it happens to be true: the UAE has no personal income tax. No income tax, no National Insurance, no capital gains tax, no tax return in the ordinary course. If you become UK non-resident, your salary is your take-home.

Here is what that means at senior-operator salaries, using the actual 2025/26 England & NI bands, including the detail most people forget: the personal allowance disappears entirely above £125,140.

Now the costs, because there are costs

Anyone who shows you the first table without this one is selling you something.

  • Rent. A good two-bed in the Marina, Downtown or similar runs roughly AED 140–220k a year (≈ £2,500–4,000 a month, approximate). That brackets a comparable London flat, so you won’t save much here. Rent is typically paid in one to four cheques a year, so you need cash up front.
  • Schools. The big one for families. There is no state option for expats; good international schools run AED 40–90k per child per year (≈ £9–20k, approximate). Two children in good schools can cost £25–35k a year that the UK gave you free.
  • Healthcare. Employer-paid insurance is standard at senior level and the private care is good. Check the policy covers your family properly before you sign, not after.
  • Pension. There is no auto-enrolment and usually no employer pension. The discipline is on you: a common approach is to redirect a fixed slice of the tax saving into your own investments every month. End-of-service gratuity exists but treat it as a bonus, not a plan.
  • Flights and the invisible line. Trips home, summers out of the heat, the premium you pay for familiar brands. Budget £5–10k a year for a family and you won’t be surprised.

The worked example

A family of four, £180,000 salary, two children in good schools, nice two-bed-plus flat. Against London: you gain roughly £73k in tax, give back £25–35k in school fees, roughly break even on rent, spend £5–10k more on flights and sundries, and should be putting £15–20k into your own pension to replace what auto-enrolment was doing. Net position: ahead by roughly £20–40k a year (approximate, and sensitive to your choices), plus whatever uplift the package itself carries.

A single person or couple without school fees at the same salary: ahead by £55–65k a year. The arithmetic is simply better the fewer school places you need.

The fine print that is actually fine print

UK non-residence is a real legal status with real rules: the Statutory Residence Test counts your UK days and ties (home, work, family). Get it wrong and HMRC still owns your worldwide income. Split-year treatment usually applies in the year you leave, and your UK rental income or UK-source gains stay taxable. None of this is hard, but it needs a proper adviser before you resign, not after. We’ll point members to good ones; we don’t give tax advice and neither should a landing page.

One more honest note: if your motivation is purely the tax, don’t come. The people who stay and thrive came for the remit and the market and treated the tax as compounding. The people who come only for the money tend to leave within two years, usually in August.
